## Purpose

This memo documents our decision regarding MegaCorp Japan's renewal negotiation and establishes precedent for future strategic account pricing decisions.

## Background

Last week, we faced a critical decision with our largest customer, MegaCorp Japan (¥25M ARR):
- Contract renewal due February 1
- Customer requested 20% discount (¥5M reduction)
- Competitive threat from WorkMax offering aggressive pricing
- Risk of losing our most visible enterprise reference

## Decision Made

After thorough analysis and stakeholder consultation, I approved:

**15% discount (¥3.75M) + ¥3M in value-adds, structured as 3-year contract**

### Breakdown:
- Year 1: ¥21.25M (15% discount)
- Year 2: ¥21.25M (locked rate)
- Year 3: ¥21.25M (locked rate)
- Total 3-year value: ¥63.75M
- Plus: Dedicated CSM, premium features, 3 custom integrations

## Rationale

This decision aligns with our core values and business objectives:

### 1. Customer First (Value #1)
MegaCorp is not just revenue—they're a strategic partner. Their success with our platform drives referrals and case studies. Losing them would damage more than ¥25M in revenue.

### 2. Long-term over Short-term (Value #3)
- **3-year commitment** reduces churn risk and provides revenue predictability
- Multi-year deals improve LTV:CAC ratio
- Locked pricing protects against future discount requests

### 3. Creative Problem-Solving
Rather than pure discount, we negotiated value-adds:
- Dedicated CSM costs us ¥800K/year but perceived value: ¥3M
- Premium features: Marginal cost ¥200K, perceived value: ¥2M
- Custom integrations: One-time cost ¥1.2M, perceived value: ¥5M

**Total cost to us**: ~¥5.75M over 3 years  
**Customer perceived value**: ¥13.75M reduction  
**Effective discount**: 17% instead of 20% requested

### 4. Precedent Management
By saying "no" to 20% and negotiating to 15%, we:
- Avoided setting expectation that we bend to all demands
- Demonstrated we value our product appropriately
- Created framework for future strategic negotiations

## Financial Impact

**Approved by CFO Raj Patel**:
- Gross margin maintained: 68% (above our 65% threshold)
- LTV calculation: ¥63.75M over 3 years vs. ¥0 if lost
- CAC payback: Already past payback, this is pure retention value

## Stakeholder Alignment

- **VP Sales (Kenji Sato)**: Initially pushed for 20%, accepted 15% + value-adds after seeing total package value
- **CFO (Raj Patel)**: Approved financial terms, emphasized margin protection
- **VP Customer Success (Miho Suzuki)**: Committed to dedicated CSM delivery
- **CTO (Yuki Nakamura)**: Approved custom integrations scope and timeline

## Lessons Learned

1. **Negotiate value, not just price**: Value-adds cost less than cash discounts but have higher perceived value
2. **Multi-year deals are strategic**: Lock in customers, reduce churn, improve predictability
3. **Know your walk-away point**: We were prepared to lose the deal rather than accept 20% (bad precedent)
4. **Speed matters**: We responded within 48 hours of their request, demonstrating we value them

## Future Implications

This decision establishes precedent (Precedent ID: DC001 in Discount Policy):

**For strategic accounts (>¥20M ARR)**:
- Maximum 15% discount on renewal
- Creative value-adds encouraged
- Multi-year commitments preferred
- CFO + CEO approval required

**This is now documented in Discount Policy v2.1 (Section 10.1)**
