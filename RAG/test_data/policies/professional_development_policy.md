## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes professional development opportunities to:
- Support employee career growth and skill development
- Retain top talent through learning investments
- Build organizational capabilities
- Promote continuous learning culture
- Provide clear career paths (IC and management tracks)

**Context**: This policy was enhanced following DC_AKIKO_003 (career development program implementation after VP Engineering departure)

---

## 2. Scope

Applies to all NexaTech employees after completing probationary period (3 months)

---

## 3. Philosophy

NexaTech believes:
- **Employees own their careers**: We provide resources and support, you drive your development
- **Learning is continuous**: Not just formal training, but daily learning opportunities
- **Multiple paths to growth**: Individual contributor (IC) and management tracks both valued
- **Investment in people**: Development budget is investment, not expense

---

## 4. Learning Budget

### 4.1 Annual Budget Per Employee

| Employee Level | Annual Budget (¥) | Examples |
|----------------|------------------|----------|
| Individual Contributor | 150,000 | Conferences, courses, books |
| Senior IC / Manager | 250,000 | Advanced training, certifications |
| Director / VP | 400,000 | Executive coaching, leadership programs |
| C-Level | 600,000 | Executive education, advisory boards |

**Note**: Budget is per calendar year, does not roll over

### 4.2 What Budget Covers

**Eligible expenses**:
- ✓ Conferences and professional events
- ✓ Online courses (Udemy, Coursera, Pluralsight, etc.)
- ✓ Professional certifications (PMP, AWS, CISSP, CPA, etc.)
- ✓ Books and learning materials
- ✓ Coaching and mentorship (external)
- ✓ Professional membership fees
- ✓ Workshops and bootcamps
- ✓ Language learning (business-relevant)
- ✓ Industry research subscriptions

**Not covered**:
- ❌ Degree programs (see Section 5)
- ❌ Personal hobbies unrelated to work
- ❌ Team building events (separate budget)
- ❌ Standard work tools/software

### 4.3 Budget Approval

**Approval required**:
- <¥50K: Manager approval
- ¥50K-150K: Manager + Department Head
- >¥150K: Manager + Head of People

**Process**:
1. Discuss with manager (align with development goals)
2. Submit request via HR system
3. Include: What, why, how it helps role/career
4. Approval within 3 business days

## 5. Formal Education Support

### 5.1 Degree Programs (MBA, MS, etc.)

**Eligibility**:
- 2+ years at NexaTech
- Strong performance (Meets/Exceeds Expectations)
- Degree relevant to current or target role
- Commitment to stay 2 years post-completion

**Support**:
- Tuition reimbursement: Up to ¥2,000,000 over 2 years
- Flexible schedule for classes (with manager approval)
- Study leave: Up to 5 days/year

**Application process**:
1. Submit proposal to manager and Head of People
2. Include: Program details, relevance to role, commitment plan
3. Decision within 30 days
4. Sign education agreement (repayment terms if leave early)

**Repayment**:
- Leave within 1 year of completion: 100% repayment
- Leave within 2 years of completion: 50% repayment
- Stay 2+ years: No repayment

### 5.2 Professional Certifications

**Fully covered** (no budget limit) for job-relevant certifications:

**Engineering**:
- AWS Certified Solutions Architect
- Google Cloud Professional
- Kubernetes certifications (CKA, CKAD)
- Security certifications (CISSP, CEH)

**Product/Design**:
- Certified Scrum Product Owner (CSPO)
- Pragmatic Marketing Certified
- UX certifications

**Sales/Marketing**:
- Salesforce certifications
- HubSpot certifications
- Google Analytics certification

**Finance**:
- CPA, CFA (study materials + exam fees)
- FP&A certifications

**Process**:
- Discuss with manager
- Company pays for exam + study materials
- Receive bonus upon passing (¥100K for major certs)
- Must pass within 2 attempts

## 6. Career Paths and Frameworks

### 6.1 Dual Career Tracks

**Individual Contributor (IC) Track**:
- Junior → Mid → Senior → Staff → Principal
- Technical/domain expertise deepens
- Influence through expertise, not management

**Management Track**:
- Manager → Senior Manager → Director → VP → C-Level
- Leadership and people management
- Influence through team building

**Compensation**: Both tracks have equivalent compensation at same levels

**Example (Engineering)**:
- IC: Junior Engineer → Engineer → Senior Engineer → Staff Engineer → Principal Engineer
- Management: Engineering Manager → Senior EM → Director of Engineering → VP Engineering → CTO

### 6.2 Career Frameworks

**Each role has defined**:
- Core competencies required
- Expected impact and scope
- Promotion criteria
- Typical timeline (but not rigid)

**Frameworks available**: [Internal career site]

**Example - Engineer to Senior Engineer**:
- **Technical skills**: Master 2+ tech stacks, system design, code review
- **Impact**: Lead medium projects, mentor 2+ juniors
- **Collaboration**: Work across teams effectively
- **Timeline**: Typically 2-3 years

### 6.3 Career Conversations

**Quarterly career 1:1s** with manager:
- Progress on current goals
- Skills to develop
- Career aspirations
- Learning opportunities
- Feedback and coaching

**Annual career planning**:
- Review past year growth
- Set development goals for next year
- Identify stretch assignments
- Discuss promotion readiness

**Manager responsibility**: Actively support employee development, not just evaluate performance

## 9. Internal Mobility

### 9.1 Internal Transfers

**Employees can apply** for open positions internally:
- Must be in current role ≥12 months
- Meets/Exceeds performance expectations
- Discuss with current manager first (transparency)

**Process**:
- Apply via internal job board
- Interview like external candidate
- Hiring manager makes decision
- If selected: 4-week transition period

**Current manager cannot block** transfer if:
- Employee meets eligibility criteria
- Hiring manager wants to hire
- But can negotiate transition timeline

### 9.2 Rotational Programs

**Engineering rotation** (for growth):
- Spend 3-6 months on different team
- Learn new tech stack or product
- Return to original team or stay (mutual decision)

**Cross-functional rotations**:
- Engineers → Product Management (trial)
- Sales → Customer Success (common path)
- Marketing → Product Marketing

**Approval**: Both managers + Head of People

### 9.3 Promotion Process

**Promotions based on**:
- Demonstrated impact at next level (not potential)
- Sustained performance (not one-time achievement)
- Role availability (business needs)

**Promotion cycle**:
- **Quarterly**: Managers nominate employees
- **Review committee**: Department head + HR + peer managers
- **Calibration**: Ensure consistency across teams
- **Approval**: VP or CEO (depending on level)
- **Effective**: Start of following quarter

**Transparency**:
- Promotion criteria documented
- Feedback if not promoted
- Development plan to reach next level

## 12. Skill Development for Role Changes

### 12.1 Upskilling for Promotions

**If targeting promotion**:
- Discuss with manager (quarterly career 1:1)
- Identify skill gaps
- Create 6-month development plan
- Manager provides stretch assignments
- Regular check-ins on progress

**Example - Engineer → Senior Engineer**:
- **Gap**: System design experience
- **Plan**: 
  - Take system design course (Udemy)
  - Shadow Staff Engineer on architecture review
  - Lead design for medium project
  - Present at internal tech talk

### 12.2 Reskilling for Career Pivots

**If changing roles** (e.g., Engineer → Product Manager):
- Assess fit (informational interviews, shadowing)
- 3-6 month learning plan
- Trial project or rotation (if possible)
- Formal application for role when ready

**Company support**:
- Manager and HR coaching
- Learning budget for relevant training
- Mentorship from target role
- Honest feedback about readiness

### 12.3 Stretch Assignments

**Growth through challenging work**:
- Lead project outside comfort zone
- Present to executives
- Manage cross-functional initiative
- Mentor junior team member
- Represent company at event

**Manager responsibility**: Provide stretch assignments appropriate to development goals

## 14. Measuring Impact

### 14.1 Development Goal Tracking

**Annual development goals** (separate from performance goals):
- 2-3 specific learning objectives
- Tracked in HR system
- Reviewed quarterly
- Progress discussed in 1:1s

**Example goals**:
- "Complete AWS Solutions Architect certification"
- "Improve public speaking (present at 3 team meetings)"
- "Learn Python for data analysis (complete 2 projects)"

### 14.2 Company-Level Metrics

**HR tracks**:
- Development budget utilization (target: 80%+)
- Internal promotion rate (target: 30% of roles)
- Employee engagement in learning (surveys)
- Retention of high performers

**Quarterly reviews**: Assess program effectiveness

### 14.3 ROI of Development

**Business benefits** we've seen:
- Lower attrition (8% vs 12% before program)
- Higher internal mobility (30% of roles filled internally)
- Faster promotion readiness
- Improved employee engagement scores
- Stronger employer brand (recruiting advantage)
